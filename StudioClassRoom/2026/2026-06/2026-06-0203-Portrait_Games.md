# Portrait Games

Why won't the photo hang straight?

by David Morton

## Article

Every morning, the same photo on the Wall of Excellence was crooked. Not just a little off - definitely tilted, like someone had tugged it to one side, annoying Mr. Potter, the janitor. The photo was one of the most easily forgettable on the wall: an unclear shot of a former assistant principal holding a plaque from 2012. It was sandwiched between a photo of a student holding up a science trophy and one of last year's champion basketball team.

"That's the one that keeps going crooked?" Ivy asked.

Mr. Potter nodded. "Every single day. I fix it and the next morning - it's off again. I have no idea why, but I've thought about taping it to the wall."

Ivy frowned. "But why this one? It's not even a photo anyone looks at."

Dean looked at the ceiling. "Give me a second." He strode down the hallway, glancing up at the school's security cameras. Then he pulled up the school's floor map on his phone and began marking angles with his finger.

"Dean?" Ivy asked.

He pointed back toward the crooked photo. "That spot is a total dead zone."

"What do you mean?"

"I mean the hallway cameras overlap just enough to cover the trophy case and the doors - except that one spot. The photo's in the small area between both angles that isn't covered. A perfect blind spot."

Ivy blinked. "So someone chose that frame for a reason."

They lingered in the hallway until it cleared out after school. Then Ivy gently lifted the frame off its hook and found what they had been anticipating: a rough hole, hidden behind the photo.

Dean reached in and pulled out a neatly folded note. "Meet me where the curtain falls. Third period."

Dean raised an eyebrow. "Who writes like this? Shakespeare's ghost?"

Ivy grinned. "Nope. Just teenagers with very dramatic taste."

The next day during third period, Ivy and Dean slipped into the auditorium and hid behind some stage sets. They didn't have to wait long.

A boy in a drama club hoodie paced nervously behind the curtain. A moment later, a girl in a debate team jacket stepped into the shadows beside him.

Dean's eyes widened. "Drama and debate? Seriously?"

Ivy nodded. "Sworn enemies. Ever since the meltdown over the auditorium schedule."

The two clubs had been fighting for months over stage time. The quarrel had gotten bad enough for the principal to get involved. However, tensions had never cooled.

Dean watched as the couple gazed deeply into each other's eyes. "Star-crossed lovers," he mumbled.

Later that afternoon, Ivy slid her own note into the wall behind the crooked frame:

Maybe pick a better hiding spot - Mr. Potter is busy enough as it is. - A friendly observer.

The next morning, the photo was hanging straight - and it stayed that way.

## Usage Tip

### Tip 1
have/has no idea

`have/has no idea` 意為「不知道」，後面可以接疑問詞引導的子句或疑問詞引導的不定詞片語，也可以接 `that` 子句。類似用法包括 `have/has no clue`、`doesn't/don't have a clue` 等。

### Tip 2
S + be/V + adj./adv. + enough + (for one) to + V

這個句型表示「足夠……以至於……」。`enough` 應置於副詞和形容詞後；若要明確指出進行不定詞動作的人，就在 `to V` 之前加上 `for one`。
