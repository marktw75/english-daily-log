## Article

If there is anywhere in the United States where you can truly experience Southern hospitality, it's Charleston, South Carolina. This coastal city blends gracious manners, a rich history and modern energy in a way few places can match. Whether you come for the food, the waterfront views or the stories that shaped the nation, Charleston offers a memorable and welcoming experience.

The city has a long and complex past and presents a host of opportunities to mix fun and relaxation with meaningful, even challenging, learning moments. One of Charleston's most significant historic sites is Fort Sumter, where the first shot of the American Civil War was fired in April 1861. Visitors today can take a boat ride across Charleston Harbor to explore the fort, now preserved as a national monument.

Of course, the best way to soak up the atmosphere is to do what Charlestonians do: walk. Wander down King Street. You'll pass little shops, cafes and classic Southern restaurants that serve specialties such as fried chicken, banana pudding and sweet tea, a popular drink of the South. Further down the street, you'll pass elegant three-story mansions - many now beautifully restored bed and breakfasts (B&Bs).

Charleston also offers countless walking tours that share deeper stories about the city's colonial era. You'll hear of its role in the Civil War and about the lives of slaves who built and sustained it. Museum homes like the Heyward-Washington House or Aiken-Rhett House offer an even closer look at 18th- and 19th-century life in the Southern U.S.

When you've had your fill of historic Charleston, drive to a nearby beach. Cross the Arthur Ravenel Jr. Bridge to reach Sullivan's Island, known for its quiet beauty, or head to Folly Beach to enjoy swimming and surfing.

No trip to Charleston would be complete without a visit to one of the region's most celebrated estates: Magnolia Plantation & Gardens. Magnolia Plantation, founded in 1676, is one of the oldest public gardens in America. It's famous for its romantic garden design and amazing variety of plants and animals. Visiting in early spring, when flowers bloom in vivid pinks and purples, is especially lovely.

Return to Folly Beach and relax with a sunset stand-up paddleboard (SUP) tour. Watch the sky change colors as the sun drops toward the horizon. It's a quiet, glowing finish to a wonderful visit to Charleston.

## Pineapple Symbol

In the 18th century, a pineapple became a symbol of hospitality and welcome. At that time, pineapples were rare and expensive in North America. Sea captains sailing from Charleston to the Caribbean would bring some back and place one outside their home. This signaled that they had arrived safely with something special. Over time, the pineapple came to represent warmth, generosity and good manners. Today, you'll see pineapples pictured on gates, fences and fountains around Charleston homes as a sign that visitors are welcome.
