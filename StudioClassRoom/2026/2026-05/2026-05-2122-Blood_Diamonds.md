# Blood Diamonds

Precious stones can fund deadly conflicts

by Elizabeth Koroma

## Article

Blood diamonds have fueled violence in several African nations, but international efforts like the Kimberley Process aim to enforce an ethical diamond trade.

Diamonds rank among the most valuable and sought-after minerals in the world. But because they are so precious, diamonds can provide motivation and funding for terrible evils. Criminals and corrupt governments sometimes exploit people to get diamonds that they can sell for profit. And some diamonds, known as blood diamonds, are sold by groups that use the profits to fuel wars.

This reality came to the world's attention in the 1990s when the West African nation of Sierra Leone experienced a brutal civil war. The nation's politicians were corrupt, using the diamond trade to enrich themselves at the nation's expense. But then a rebel group called the Revolutionary United Front (RUF) invaded the nation, attempting to take control of the government. The RUF quickly seized several diamond mining areas and proceeded to use them to fund its war. The profits this group gained from selling the diamonds prolonged the civil war, with devastating consequences for Sierra Leone's people. The RUF killed many civilians and injured others, even cutting off people's limbs to instill terror and show that the government could not protect its own people.

Similar conflicts occurred in other diamond-rich African countries as well. Often the rebel groups would capture people and compel them to mine diamonds, so these diamonds were often obtained with slave labor.

To restrict the blood-diamond trade, the international community has taken action, with many nations prohibiting imports from countries where diamond profits fuel war. Sixty nations have joined the Kimberley Process, nations that have pledged to be accountable for how they engage in the diamond trade. Member nations commit to enforcing strict standards for mining and trading diamonds and keeping their own actions in the diamond trade transparent.

Member nations also agree to buy and sell diamonds only with nations that are part of the Kimberley Process. The Kimberley Process Certification Scheme (KPCS) involves issuing every diamond a certificate confirming that it was mined, cut and traded in an ethical manner.

The Kimberley Process does have some weaknesses: It can be difficult to enforce, and corrupt officials can falsely claim that some diamonds' origins are free from conflict. Additionally, some groups argue that the Kimberley Process defines blood diamonds too narrowly, causing important problems to be overlooked. Some jewelers, however, take steps beyond those required by the Kimberley Process to ensure that their diamonds come from ethical sources.

The more wealth an industry produces, the more corruption it inspires. Thus, it is no surprise that the diamond trade has been used for evil. Progress is uneven, but international pressure has made it harder for blood diamonds to be moved around freely to fund violence.

## Key Point

- Blood diamonds can finance war and exploitation, so international efforts like the Kimberley Process try to limit the trade through ethical standards and certification.

## Usage Tip

### Tip 1
known as ...

`known as` 表示「以……聞名；被稱為……」，常用來補充說明人、事、物的名稱、身分或特徵。它是過去分詞片語，通常放在名詞後面作補充說明。類似說法還有 `recognized as`、`regarded as`。例如：Leonardo da Vinci, regarded as a genius artist and inventor, influenced many fields of study. 李奧納多．達文西被視為天才藝術家與發明家，影響了許多研究領域。

### Tip 2
The more ..., the more ...

這個句型用來表達「越……，就越……」的對應關係。前後兩個比較級子句彼此呼應，說明一方變化會帶動另一方變化。句中的比較級可以是形容詞、副詞，或搭配名詞、動詞使用。例如：The more effort you put into your work, the more success you will achieve. 你在工作上投入越多努力，就會獲得越多成功。
