# Mysterious Glowing Waters

How this phenomenon works and where to see it

by Christine Bedwell

## Article

The magical-looking phenomenon of blue-green shining waters is created when energy is turned into light by living things. Puerto Rico is a top destination to witness these glowing waters, but they can also be spotted along coastlines around the world on dark nights.

A young moon reflects a faint light as a cluster of people in small boats paddle quietly through a dark bay. Each paddle stroke stirs up a bright blue-green glow as if billions of stars had fallen into the water's depths.

What these people are seeing is bioluminescence, light produced through a chemical reaction inside millions of tiny living organisms. They thrive in small, warm, calm bodies of water near the ocean and vitamin B12-rich mangrove forests. When the water is disturbed by a wave, a boat or even a hand, the organisms light up, creating sparkling trails in the dark.

Bioluminescence, meaning "living light," happens when organisms mix chemical compounds called luciferins with oxygen. The reaction releases energy in the form of light, much like how a firefly glows.

Puerto Rico, a prime location to witness this phenomenon, boasts three flourishing bioluminescent ecosystems, each in an area with minimal light pollution. The *Guinness Book of World Records* recognizes the Caribbean island's Mosquito Bay as the brightest. From December to mid-April, tourists flock to the town of Esperanza on Vieques Island for guided night tours across its glittering waters. Laguna Grande, near San Juan on the main island, is the most traveler-friendly location to experience bioluminescent waters, while swimming is permitted in La Parguera's glowing waters off the island's southwest coast.

Glowing waters aren't found only in Puerto Rico. At Luminous Lagoon in Jamaica, where the fresh-water Martha Brae River and the salty Caribbean Sea meet, conditions are perfect for the waters to twinkle. Tours there offer a boat ride, a magical swim and a fire show for a night that feels out of this world. For a more serene experience, travelers can head to the other side of the globe and wander along Vietnam's Cat Ba Island beach to see sparkling waters.

From April to September, people also flock to the Matsu Islands of Beigan and Nangan to see the Blue Tears of Matsu. These islands are considered one of the most beautiful places in Asia to see bioluminescent water.

Even the waters along U.S. and Mexican shores brighten on occasion, but the appearance of bioluminescence depends on environmental factors that affect intensity and frequency.

These glowing waters are fragile ecosystems. The plankton that create the light are sensitive to pollution, chemicals and changes in water temperature or salinity. Overtourism can also harm them. Sunscreen, boat fuel and even lights from nearby towns can potentially reduce their glow. For this reason, local governments and environmental groups have placed restrictions on swimming and boating in many bioluminescent bays. Visitors are often encouraged to take boats without engines and to avoid wearing lotions or sprays that might harm the organisms.

Protecting bioluminescent ecosystems ensures future travelers can witness the quiet wonder of nature's living light show. In the darkest places, life itself becomes a source of light, a glowing reminder that science and magic are often one and the same.

## Key Point

- Bioluminescent waters appear when tiny organisms emit light after the water is stirred, creating glowing trails in the dark.
- Puerto Rico is famous for this phenomenon, but similar glowing waters can also be seen in Jamaica, Vietnam, the Matsu Islands and other coastlines around the world.
- Because these ecosystems are fragile, many places limit swimming, boating and pollution to preserve their natural glow.

## Usage Tip

### Tip 1
as if + S + had + p.p. ...

這個句型用來表示說話者覺得某人或某事「看起來好像已經……了」，通常帶有與事實相反或不確定真假的語氣。`as if` 後面接假設語氣，若描述的是比現在更早發生的情況，就常用 `had + p.p.`。例如：`She looked as if she had been hit by a car.` 她看起來好像被車撞過一樣。這種用法常見於描述外表、語氣或行為給人的感覺。

### Tip 2
glow-in-the-dark / made-to-order / up-to-date

這些都是常見的複合形容詞，用連字號把多個字連起來，一起修飾後面的名詞。`glow-in-the-dark` 表示「會在黑暗中發光的」，例如 `glow-in-the-dark stickers` 夜光貼紙；`made-to-order` 表示「訂做的」，例如 `made-to-order shoes` 訂製鞋；`up-to-date` 表示「最新的」，例如 `up-to-date information` 最新資訊。遇到這類連字號結構時，可以把它們當作一個完整形容詞來理解。
