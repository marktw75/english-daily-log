# Jane Goodall: Voice of the Wild

This inspiring pioneer lived among chimpanzees and changed the world's opinion of them

by Pamela Osment

## Article

Armed with bravery and curiosity, with a notebook in hand, Jane Goodall transformed a childhood dream into a remarkable discovery.

When Goodall was young, she imagined going to Africa and living among the wild animals. Never could she have guessed that not only would her dream come true, but she would set a new scientific standard for animal behavior research.

Her mother encouraged her to go to Africa, but Goodall faced many obstacles. She had no scientific training, no degree, and she was female. But fate intervened. In 1957, at age 23, Goodall accepted a friend's invitation to visit Africa. There, she met the famous archaeologist Dr. Louis Leakey. He requested that she come work for him at the natural history museum in Nairobi, Kenya. Dr. Leakey quickly discovered that Goodall had a quality essential for all field researchers: a vast reserve of patience.

This led to an extraordinary proposal. Would Goodall go and live among the chimpanzees in East Africa and observe their daily lives? She excitedly agreed, and in July 1960, she arrived in Tanzania at the Gombe Stream Game Reserve, anxious for her adventure to begin. Before long, the young researcher would make a groundbreaking discovery.

During Goodall's first few months in the forest, the chimps would run from her. But they soon became accustomed to her presence, ignored her and resumed their daily activities.

On one occasion, Goodall observed an older ape repeatedly putting a blade of grass into a termite hole, removing termites and eating them. This was a significant discovery because, up to this point, the scientific community believed only humans created and used tools.

Goodall was gradually accepted into the chimps' society, carefully interacting with them. Over the years, she found them to be intelligent, emotional animals living in complex social communities.

In the 1980s, Goodall became aware of a global decrease in animal habitats, including the Gombe region. She decided to become a full-time advocate for wildlife, traveling more than 300 days a year. Goodall spoke to international groups, encouraging them to protect animals and their environment. If it had not been for her recognition of wildlife's decreasing territory, her international impact might never have been felt.

Distinguished scientist and conservationist Jane Goodall passed away on October 1, 2025. Yet her life continues to inspire millions of people around the world to protect the planet and its precious wonders.

## Usage Tip

### Tip 1
If it had not been for + N/V-ing, S + would/could/might have + p.p.

用來表達「要不是……，事情本來就會有不同結果」，表示與過去事實相反的假設語氣。也常用 `Had it not been for ...` 來省略 `if`，語氣更正式。若要表達「與現在事實相反」時，則可改用 `If it were not for ...`。例如：If it were not for your help, I couldn't finish the project in time. 要不是你的幫忙，我就無法及時完成這個專案。
